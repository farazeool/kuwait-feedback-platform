# 1. Introduction

## What is this system?

Imagine you own a restaurant in Kuwait. Every day, customers come in, eat your food, and leave. Some customers are happy. Some are not. But mostly, they just leave without telling you what they thought.

This system is like a **digital suggestion box** that never gets lost, never gets ignored, and automatically organizes all customer feedback so you can actually use it to make your business better.

The full name is the **Kuwait Feedback Platform**. But inside this guide, we'll just call it **"the system"** or **"the platform"**.

## What problem does it solve?

Before this system existed:

- When customers wanted to give feedback, they might tell a staff member, who might forget to write it down.
- Paper suggestion boxes collected dust. Nobody read them.
- If one branch of a restaurant was doing badly, the owner wouldn't know until it was too late.
- If a staff member was especially good (or bad) with customers, nobody kept track of that.
- There was no way to compare how different locations were performing.

After using this system:

- Customers can give feedback instantly by scanning a QR code with their phone.
- The feedback goes straight into the system, organized by date, location, and rating.
- Managers get automatic alerts when something is wrong (like a very low rating).
- You can see charts showing if your business is getting better or worse over time.
- You can compare different branches side-by-side.

## Who will use it?

| Person | What they do in the system |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **Customers** | Scan a QR code, answer a short survey, submit feedback |
| **Staff/Employees** | Give customers the QR code to scan, sometimes have their own personal feedback link |
| **Location Managers** | Watch their branch's ratings, respond to alerts, track employee performance |
| **Organization Admins** | Create surveys, view all locations' data, manage team members |
| **Organization Owners** | Full control over everything — settings, billing, team, branding |
| **Platform Admins** | Super-admins who can see across all organizations (only internal platform staff) |

## What kind of business is this for?

This system was designed for businesses in Kuwait that have **multiple locations** or **branches**. For example:

- A restaurant chain with branches in Salmiya, Sharq, and other areas
- A retail store with multiple showrooms
- A healthcare clinic with several branches
- A service center (like a car repair shop) with multiple locations

## A simple analogy

Think of this system like a **suggestion box + automatic report card + alarm system** all in one:

- **Suggestion box**: Customers tell you what they think
- **Report card**: The system automatically calculates scores and grades for each location
- **Alarm system**: When something bad happens (a very low rating), the system alerts the right person immediately

# 2. System Overview

## The complete picture

Let's walk through how the entire system works, from start to finish, using a real example.

### Example: "A customer has lunch at a restaurant in Salmiya"

**Step 1: The restaurant prepares**

Before customers even arrive, the restaurant admin has already:
- Created an account in the system for their organization
- Set up their two locations (Salmiya and Sharq)
- Created a customer satisfaction survey with questions like "How was your experience?" (rating 1-5), "What did you enjoy?" (multiple choice), and "Any suggestions?" (text)
- Published the survey so it's live
- Printed a QR code card and placed it on each table

**Step 2: The customer arrives**

A customer named Sara comes in for lunch. She has a good meal. When she's done, she sees a small card on the table that says:

"Scan this QR code to tell us about your visit!"

**Step 3: The customer gives feedback**

Sara scans the QR code with her phone camera. Her phone opens a web page showing the survey. She:
1. Rates her experience 4 out of 5 stars
2. Selects "Food quality" as what she enjoyed most
3. Types: "The food was delicious but the service was a bit slow"
4. Clicks Submit

**Step 4: The system records the response**

Behind the scenes, the system:
- Records Sara's answers
- Notes which location she visited (Salmiya)
- Records that it came from the QR code channel
- Logs the date and time
- Calculates the normalized score (4 out of 5 = 80%)
- Checks if the rating is low enough to trigger an alert (it's not, 4 is good)

**Step 5: The manager sees the result**

The next morning, Ahmed (the Salmiya location manager) logs into the system and sees:
- His dashboard shows all recent responses
- The average rating for Salmiya this week is 4.2 out of 5
- Sara's response is listed with her comments
- He also gets an analytics page with charts showing trends over time

**Step 6: The admin sees the big picture**

A week later, Fatima (the organization admin) logs in and sees:
- The dashboard comparing both locations
- Salmiya average: 4.2, Sharq average: 4.7
- A chart showing response trends over the last 30 days
- A chart showing how ratings are distributed
- Which survey questions are getting the best/worst responses

## What happens without this system?

Without this system, the restaurant owner would:
- Have no idea how customers really feel (most don't complain directly)
- Not know if Salmiya is doing worse than Sharq until customers stop coming
- Have no record of which employees are performing well
- Have no automatic way to be alerted about problems
- Rely on word-of-mouth and guesses

## What improves after using this system?

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| No customer feedback (or very little) | Regular feedback from every visit |
| No way to compare locations | Side-by-side location comparison |
| Problems discovered too late | Instant alerts for low ratings |
| No record of employee performance | Employee attribution via distribution links |
| Guesswork for business decisions | Data-driven decisions with charts and analytics |
| Customers leave without telling you | Easy QR-code feedback anytime |
| Mixed-up paper records | Organized digital database |

## Major parts of the system

Let's look at the main sections of the system:

### Dashboard (Home Page)
When you log in, this is the first page you see. It shows:
- A summary of all recent feedback
- Charts showing trends
- Alert counts
- KPI (Key Performance Indicator) cards

### Surveys
Think of a survey as a **digital questionnaire**. You create questions, publish them, and customers answer them. Each survey belongs to a specific location.

### Responses
When a customer submits a survey, their answers become a "response." You can see all responses, filter them, and take action on them.

### Alerts
An alert is like a **warning light**. If a customer gives a very low rating (like 1 or 2 out of 5), the system creates an alert so the manager knows something went wrong.

### Analytics
This is the **report card** section. It shows charts, graphs, and numbers that tell you how your business is doing.

### KPI Dashboard
KPI stands for Key Performance Indicator. These are the most important numbers for your business, like average rating, satisfaction percentage, and response count.

### Team
This section manages who can use the system at your organization. You can add people, remove people, and control what they can see.

### Settings
This is where you configure your organization's name, branding (logo, colors), locations, departments, and other preferences.

# 3. User Roles

## The five types of users

The system has five different roles. Each role can see and do different things.

### 1. Platform Admin (Super Admin)

**Who they are:** The people who run the platform itself. This is usually someone from the company that created the software.

**What they can do:**
- See ALL organizations in the system
- View platform-wide analytics
- Access audit logs
- Manage any organization

**What they CANNOT do:**
- Read individual customer survey answers (for privacy)
- Create surveys for organizations

**Real-world comparison:** Like a building manager who has keys to every room but doesn't enter them without permission.

---

### 2. Organization Owner

**Who they are:** The business owner. The person who started the company.

**What they can do:**
- Everything within their own organization
- Transfer ownership to someone else
- Delete the organization

**Real-world comparison:** Like the owner of a restaurant chain. They can walk into any branch, check anything, and make any decision.

---

### 3. Organization Admin

**Who they are:** A senior employee who manages day-to-day operations.

**What they can do:**
- Create and publish surveys
- View all locations' data
- Manage team members (add/remove people, but cannot transfer ownership)
- Manage settings and branding
- View all analytics

**What they CANNOT do:**
- Transfer ownership
- Delete the organization

**Real-world comparison:** Like a general manager who runs the business day-to-day for the owner.

---

### 4. Location Manager

**Who they are:** The person in charge of ONE specific branch or location.

**What they can do:**
- See responses from their assigned location(s) only
- Respond to alerts for their location
- View analytics for their location only
- See the pilot dashboard

**What they CANNOT do:**
- See data from other locations
- Create or publish surveys
- Manage team members
- Compare locations across the organization

**Real-world comparison:** Like a shift supervisor who is responsible for one restaurant branch.

---

### 5. Analyst

**Who they are:** A data person who needs to view reports but shouldn't make changes.

**What they can do:**
- View responses (read-only)
- View analytics and charts
- View the dashboard

**What they CANNOT do:**
- Create or modify anything
- Manage team members
- Change settings
- Update response workflows

**Real-world comparison:** Like a business consultant who looks at numbers and gives advice but doesn't make operational changes.

# 4. Getting Started

## Your first time using the system

### Step 1: Log in

1. Open your web browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge, etc.)
2. Go to the web address your administrator gave you (for example: https://feedback.yourcompany.com)
3. You will see a login page
4. Type your email address
5. Type your password
6. Click the **Sign in** button

**What if you don't have an account yet?**
- Your organization admin must invite you. They will send you an email with a special invitation link.
- Click the link in the email to accept the invitation and create your account.

### Step 2: Look at the dashboard

After logging in, you will see the **Dashboard**. This is your home page.

On the dashboard you will see:
- **Pilot Checklist** (if you're a new organization): A list of steps to complete before you can collect feedback
- **Feedback overview charts**: Graphs showing response data
- **KPI cards**: Numbers showing total responses, average rating, etc.
- **Recent responses table**: A list of the most recent customer feedback

### Step 3: Navigate the system

On the left side (or top, on mobile phones), there is a navigation menu. The items in the menu depend on your role.

**Common menu items:**

| Menu Item | What it does |
|-----------|-------------|
| Dashboard | Home page with overview |
| Surveys | Create and manage your feedback questionnaires |
| Responses | See all customer answers |
| Alerts | See warnings about problems |
| Analytics | Charts and data reports |
| KPI | Key performance indicators |
| Reports | Generate detailed reports |
| Team | Manage who has access |
| Settings | Configure your organization |
| Account | Your personal profile and password |

### Step 4: Create your first survey (for admins)

To start collecting feedback, you need a survey:

1. Click **Surveys** in the menu
2. Click the **New Survey** button
3. Choose a **template** (or start from scratch)
4. Give your survey a name (e.g., "Customer Satisfaction Survey")
5. Add questions (rating, multiple choice, text)
6. Select which location the survey is for
7. Click **Save** and then **Publish**

### Step 5: Share the survey with customers

Once your survey is published, you get a **public link** and a **QR code**:

1. Open your published survey
2. Click **Distribution** or **View QR Code**
3. Download the QR code as an image (SVG or PNG)
4. Print it and place it at your location (on tables, at the counter, etc.)

### Step 6: See responses come in

When customers scan the QR code and submit feedback:
1. The response appears on your **Dashboard**
2. It also appears in the **Responses** section
3. You can click on any response to see the full details

# 5. Feature Documentation

## 5.1 Dashboard

### What is it?
The dashboard is the **home page** you see every time you log in. It gives you a quick summary of everything happening in your business.

### Why does it exist?
Instead of clicking through many pages to find out what's going on, the dashboard brings the most important information to one place.

### What problem does it solve?
Without the dashboard, you would have to check surveys, responses, alerts, and reports separately. The dashboard saves time by showing the essentials at a glance.

### Who uses it?
Everyone who logs in — but managers see only their location's data, while admins see everything.

### When would someone use it?
- Every morning to check overnight results
- Throughout the day to monitor performance
- When training new staff to show them how things work

### What you see on the dashboard

| Section | What it shows |
|---------|---------------|
| Pilot Checklist | A to-do list for new organizations (set up location, create survey, publish, collect response) |
| Response Trend Chart | A bar chart showing how many responses came in each day/week/month |
| Rating Distribution Chart | A bar chart showing how many 1-star, 2-star, 3-star, 4-star, 5-star ratings you received |
| Survey Comparison | A chart comparing different surveys' average ratings |
| Location Comparison | A chart comparing different locations' average ratings |
| Low-Score Trend | A chart showing how low ratings (3 or below out of 5) have changed over time |
| KPI Cards | Boxes showing Total Responses, Responses in Range, Average Rating, Low-Score Count, Open Alerts |
| Recent Responses Table | A list of the most recent customer feedback with rating, survey name, location, and workflow status |

## 5.2 Surveys

### What is it?
A survey is a **set of questions** you create to collect feedback from customers.

### Why does it exist?
Without surveys, customers have no structured way to give you feedback. Surveys make it easy for customers to tell you what they think.

### What problem does it solve?
- Random verbal feedback is forgotten. Surveys record everything.
- Staff might filter out negative feedback. Surveys go directly to management.
- Different locations can use the same survey so you can compare them.

### Who uses it?
Admins and Owners create and publish surveys.

### Types of questions you can add

| Question Type | What it does | Example |
|---------------|-------------|---------|
| **Rating** | Customer picks a number (e.g., 1-5 stars) | "How was your experience?" |
| **Multiple Choice** | Customer picks from options | "What did you enjoy? (Staff, Food, Cleanliness)" |
| **Text** | Customer types their own answer | "Any suggestions for improvement?" |

### Step-by-step: Creating a survey

1. Click **Surveys** in the menu
2. Click **New Survey**
3. Choose a **template** (pre-made question sets for cafes, restaurants, retail, etc.) or start **blank**
4. Enter a **name** for the survey (in English and optionally Arabic)
5. Add a **description** (optional)
6. Select the **location** this survey is for
7. Click **Add Question** to add your first question
8. Choose the question type (Rating, Multiple Choice, or Text)
9. Type the question in English and Arabic
10. For rating questions: set the minimum and maximum values (e.g., 1 to 5)
11. For multiple choice: add the options customers can pick from
12. Click **Save Draft** to save without publishing, or **Publish** to make it live immediately

### Survey statuses

| Status | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **Draft** | You are still working on it. Customers cannot see it yet. |
| **Active** | The survey is published and customers can submit responses. |
| **Archived** | The survey is closed. New responses are not accepted, but old responses are kept. |

### Survey Templates

When creating a new survey, you can choose from pre-made templates. These are ready-to-use surveys designed for specific types of businesses:

| Template | Questions included |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Cafe / Restaurant** | Rating (1-5), food quality, service speed, atmosphere, comments |
| **Retail** | Rating (1-5), product selection, staff helpfulness, store cleanliness, comments |
| **Service Center** | Rating (1-5), wait time, service quality, professionalism, comments |
| **General** | Rating (1-5), overall satisfaction, recommendation likelihood, comments |
| **Blank** | Start with no questions — add your own |

## 5.3 Responses

### What is it?
A "response" is one customer's completed survey. It contains their answers to all the questions.

### Why does it exist?
Responses are the **raw data** of your feedback system. They are the actual customer opinions that you need to read and act on.

### What problem does it solve?
- Scattered verbal feedback becomes organized, searchable records
- You can track responses over time
- You can filter responses by rating, date, location, and other criteria

### Who uses it?
Admins, managers (their own location), and analysts (read-only).

### What you see in a response

When you click on a single response, you see:
- **Rating**: The customer's score (e.g., 4 out of 5, or 80% normalized)
- **Answers**: Their answers to each question
- **Location**: Which branch they visited
- **Submitted at**: Date and time
- **Channel**: How they submitted (QR code, web link, kiosk, etc.)
- **Workflow status**: Whether the response has been reviewed or needs action

### Response Workflow Statuses

| Status | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **Monitor Only** | Just watching — no action needed right now |
| **Branch Follow-up** | The location manager needs to follow up on this |
| **Controlled Investigation** | A formal investigation has been opened |
| **Immediate Escalation** | Serious problem — needs immediate attention |

### Step-by-step: Viewing and managing responses

1. Click **Responses** in the menu
2. You'll see a list of all responses, newest first
3. Click on any response to see its full details
4. To update the workflow status:
   - Click the **Update Workflow** button
   - Select a new status
   - Optionally assign it to someone
   - Add any notes (private, only visible to staff)
   - Save

## 5.4 Alerts

### What is it?
An alert is an automatic **warning** created when a customer gives a very low rating.

### Why does it exist?
If a customer has a terrible experience and rates you 1 out of 5, you want to know about it **immediately** — not a week later when you're looking at reports.

### What problem does it solve?
- Problems are caught early, before they get worse
- Managers are notified automatically — no need to check every response manually
- Alerts can be assigned to specific people so nothing falls through the cracks

### Who uses it?
Admins, owners, and location managers.

### Alert statuses

| Status | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **Open** | The alert was just created. Nobody has looked at it yet. |
| **Acknowledged** | Someone has seen the alert and is working on it. |
| **Resolved** | The problem has been fixed. |
| **Dismissed** | The alert was reviewed but no action was needed. |

### Step-by-step: Handling an alert

1. Click **Alerts** in the menu
2. You'll see a list of alerts, newest first
3. Click an alert to see details (which response triggered it, the rating, the message)
4. To update the alert:
   - Click **Acknowledge** to show you're working on it
   - Click **Resolve** when the problem is fixed
   - Click **Dismiss** if it's not a real issue
   - You can also **assign** the alert to a team member

### How alerts are created

Alerts are created automatically when a survey response has a rating at or below a certain threshold (usually 3 out of 5). You can configure the threshold in Settings > Alert Configuration.

## 5.5 Analytics

### What is it?
Analytics is the **report card section** of the system. It shows charts, graphs, and numbers that help you understand how your business is doing.

### Why does it exist?
Looking at individual responses one at a time doesn't tell you the big picture. Analytics helps you spot trends, compare locations, and make informed decisions.

### What problem does it solve?
- Raw numbers don't tell a story. Charts and graphs do.
- Without analytics, you'd have to manually count responses and calculate averages.
- Analytics automatically normalizes ratings so you can compare different scales fairly (e.g., 1-5 vs 1-10).

### Who uses it?
Everyone, but managers see only their location.

### What you see in Analytics

| Section | What it shows |
|---------|---------------|
| **All-time responses** | Total number of responses ever collected |
| **Responses in range** | Responses within the selected date range |
| **Average rating** | The average of all ratings, normalized to 0-100% |
| **Low-score responses** | How many responses scored 40% or below |
| **Open alerts** | How many alerts need attention |
| **Response trend** | A chart showing responses per day/week/month |
| **Rating distribution** | A chart showing how ratings are spread across score bands |
| **Survey comparison** | Which surveys are getting the best/worst ratings |
| **Location comparison** | Which locations are performing best |
| **Low-score trend** | How low ratings have changed over time |
| **Recent responses** | A table of the most recent feedback |

### Filters

You can filter analytics by:
- **Date range**: Today, 7 days, 30 days, 90 days, this month, last month, or custom dates
- **Location**: See data for one branch
- **Survey**: See data for one specific survey
- **Rating range**: Only show responses within certain ratings
- **Alert status**: Only show responses that triggered alerts

### About normalized ratings

Different surveys might use different rating scales (1-5, 1-10, etc.). The system **normalizes** all ratings to a 0-100 scale so you can compare them fairly.

For example:
- A 4 out of 5 = 80%
- A 7 out of 10 = 70%
- An 8 out of 10 = 80%

Now you can compare ratings from different surveys because they're all on the same 0-100 scale.

## 5.7 Quick Feedback (One-Tap Rating)

### What is it?
A super-simple way for customers to give feedback with just **one tap**. No forms, no typing. Just tap a smiley face (😊 😐 😡) and done.

### Why does it exist?
Long surveys can be annoying for customers who just want to say "good job" or "bad experience" quickly.

### What problem does it solve?
- Increases response rates (more people give feedback)
- Better for busy customers who don't have time for long surveys
- Captures feedback that would otherwise be lost

### How it works

1. The customer scans a QR code or taps a link
2. They see emoji faces ranging from 😊 (great) to 😡 (terrible)
3. They tap the face that matches their experience
4. They can optionally add a comment or select a category
5. Done! The whole thing takes 2 seconds

### When to use Quick Feedback vs Full Survey

| Use Quick Feedback when... | Use Full Survey when... |
|---------------------------|------------------------|
| Customer is in a hurry | Customer has time to give detailed feedback |
| You just want a general mood check | You need specific answers to specific questions |
| At a kiosk or busy counter | For email follow-ups or less busy times |

## 5.8 Distribution (How Feedback Links Reach Customers)

### What is it?
Distribution is the system for **creating and managing feedback links** that you share with customers. Each link can be tracked to see who shared it and whether customers used it.

### Why does it exist?
Not all feedback is equal. If Employee A shares their feedback link and gets 50 responses while Employee B only gets 5, you know Employee A is doing a better job engaging customers.

### How it works

1. Admin creates a **distribution template** (a reusable link design for a specific channel like QR code, email, or WhatsApp)
2. Admin creates **distribution assignments** — specific links for:
   - A specific employee (employee attribution)
   - A specific location (location attribution)
   - A specific touchpoint (like a checkout counter or service desk)
3. Each assignment gets a unique link/token
4. When a customer clicks the link and submits feedback, the response is automatically attributed to that employee, location, or touchpoint

### What you can track

| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|------------------|
| Click count | How many people clicked the link |
| Response count | How many people completed the survey |
| Conversion rate | What percentage of clicks became responses |
| Employee attribution | Which employee's link got the most responses |

## 5.9 Touchpoints

### What is it?
A touchpoint is a **specific place or moment** where customers interact with your business. For example:
- The front counter / cash register
- A service desk
- A drive-through window
- A specific department (like the bakery section in a supermarket)

### Why does it exist?
If you have different departments or service points, you want to know which one is doing well and which needs improvement.

### How it works

1. An admin creates touchpoints for their locations
2. Each touchpoint can have its own feedback link
3. When a customer gives feedback through a touchpoint link, the response is tagged with that touchpoint
4. Analytics can then show which touchpoints are performing best

### Real-world example

A supermarket has three departments: Grocery, Bakery, and Butcher. Each department has a QR code at its counter. When customers give feedback through the Bakery QR code, the system knows the feedback is about the Bakery department specifically.

---

## 5.10 Departments

### What is it?
A department is a **sub-group** within a location. For example, a restaurant might have a Kitchen department and a Service department.

### Why does it exist?
Sometimes you need to know not just which location is having problems, but which department within that location.

### How it differs from a Touchpoint
- A **touchpoint** is a physical place where customers give feedback (a specific counter or desk)
- A **department** is an organizational unit (a team or section)

---

## 5.11 Campaigns

### What is it?
A campaign is a **targeted feedback collection effort**. For example, sending an email to recent customers asking them to fill out a survey.

### Why does it exist?
Instead of waiting for customers to find your QR code, campaigns let you actively reach out to customers.

### How it works

1. Admin creates a campaign
2. Associates it with a survey and a channel (email, WhatsApp, etc.)
3. The system generates links for the campaign
4. Responses are tagged with the campaign so you can measure its effectiveness

## 5.12 Export

### What is it?
Export lets you download your data as a **CSV file** (which opens in Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets).

### Why does it exist?
Sometimes you need to analyze data outside the system — for example, to create custom reports or share data with a consultant.

### How to export

1. Go to the **Dashboard**
2. Click the **Export Responses CSV** button
3. The system generates a file and downloads it
4. Open the file in Excel or Google Sheets

### What's included in the export
- Response IDs
- Ratings
- Answers
- Submission dates
- Location names
- Survey names

## 5.20 Escalation Rules

### What is it?
Escalation rules are **automatic triggers** that take action when certain conditions are met (like a very negative response that mentions specific keywords).

### Why does it exist?
Some problems are too urgent to wait for a manager to check their dashboard. Escalation rules make the system act immediately.

### How it works

1. An admin creates an escalation rule like:
   - **If**: Rating is 1 out of 5 AND the comment contains "food poisoning"
   - **Then**: Create an alert AND notify the manager immediately

2. When a matching response comes in, the rule triggers automatically

### Trigger types

| Trigger | What it checks |
|---------|---------------|
| **Rating Threshold** | Rating is at or below a specific number |
| **Keywords** | The comment contains specific words (e.g., "rude", "broken", "unsafe") |
| **Negative Sentiment** | The overall tone of the response is negative |

## 5.21 Corrective Actions

### What is it?
A corrective action is a formal **task to fix a problem** that was identified through customer feedback.

### Why does it exist?
Some problems can't be fixed with a quick follow-up. They need a formal process — an investigation, a plan to fix the issue, and verification that the fix worked.

### How it works

1. A problem is identified (through an alert, an investigation, or a manager noticing a pattern)
2. Someone creates a **corrective action** — a formal record of what went wrong and what needs to be done
3. The corrective action goes through stages:
   - **Open**: Problem identified, not yet fixed
   - **In Progress**: Work is being done to fix it
   - **Under Review**: Fix is done, being checked
   - **Closed**: Fix is verified and complete
4. You can attach **evidence** (photos, documents) to show the fix
5. Someone reviews the effectiveness of the fix

### Corrective Action Priority

| Priority | What it means |
|----------|---------------|
| **Low** | Minor issue, fix when convenient |
| **Medium** | Should be fixed soon |
| **High** | Needs attention quickly |
| **Critical** | Must be fixed immediately |

---

## 5.22 Investigations

### What is it?
An investigation is a formal **inquiry into a problem**. It's more detailed than a corrective action because you're still figuring out what happened.

### Why does it exist?
Sometimes you know something went wrong but you're not sure why. An investigation helps you find the root cause.

### How it works

1. An investigation is created (sometimes automatically from an escalation)
2. Evidence is collected (photos, documents, statements)
3. The investigation determines the root cause
4. Based on the findings, corrective actions may be created
5. The investigation is closed when the findings are complete

---

## 5.23 Evidence

### What is it?
Evidence is **proof** that something happened. It could be a photo, a PDF document, a training record, a maintenance log, or any other file.

### Why does it exist?
When you say "we fixed the broken air conditioner," you need proof. Evidence attaches files to investigations and corrective actions so you can show what was done.

### Types of evidence

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| Photo | A picture of the repaired equipment |
| PDF | A signed report |
| Checklist | A completed inspection checklist |
| Training Record | A record showing a staff member completed training |
| Maintenance Record | A service receipt |
| Supplier Document | A delivery confirmation |
| Other | Any other document |

# 8. Hardware Integration

## What hardware does this system work with?

The Kuwait Feedback Platform is a **web-based system**. It doesn't require special hardware — it works on any device that can browse the internet.

### 1. Computers and Laptops

**What they do:** Used by admins, managers, and staff to manage the system.

**How they connect:** Any computer with a web browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge, Firefox) can access the system through the internet.

**What you need:**
- Internet connection
- Web browser
- Screen (any size — the system works on both desktop and laptop)

**What if it fails:** Try a different browser. Clear your browser cache. Check your internet connection.

### 2. Smartphones (Customers)

**What they do:** Customers use their phones to scan QR codes and submit feedback.

**How they connect:** The customer opens their phone camera, scans the QR code, and the phone opens the survey in a web browser.

**What you need:**
- A phone with a camera
- Internet connection on the phone

**No app needed:** Customers do NOT need to download an app. Everything works through the phone's web browser.

**What if it fails:** Make sure the QR code is clean and well-lit. The customer can also type the survey URL directly into their browser.

### 3. Tablets (Kiosk Mode)

**What they do:** A tablet mounted at the business location lets customers tap their rating without using their own phone.

**How they connect:** The tablet opens the kiosk page in a browser and stays on that page all day.

**Setup:**
1. Get a tablet (iPad, Android tablet, or any tablet with a web browser)
2. Open the kiosk URL (e.g., https://feedback.yourcompany.com/kiosk/survey-name)
3. The page shows large, easy-to-tap buttons
4. After each response, the page automatically resets

**What if it fails:** Refresh the page. Check internet connection. Restart the tablet.

### 4. QR Code Cards (Printed)

**What they do:** Printed cards with QR codes placed on tables, counters, or walls.

**How they work:** The system generates a QR code image. You download it, print it, and display it.

**Best practices:**
- Print on card stock (thicker paper)
- Laminate for durability
- Place at eye level
- Ensure good lighting for scanning
- Test the QR code before printing many copies
- Include simple instructions in English and Arabic near the QR code

### 5. Receipt Printers (Optional)

**What they do:** Some businesses might print QR codes on customer receipts using a thermal printer.

**How it works:** If your point-of-sale system can print a custom footer on receipts, you can include the survey URL or a small QR code.

### 6. Network Equipment

**What it does:** Router, modem, and internet connection.

**Why it matters:** The system is online. Without internet, customers cannot submit feedback through QR codes, and staff cannot access the dashboard.

**What if it fails:**
- Responses are NOT lost — they simply cannot be submitted until the internet comes back
- Staff can still write down feedback manually and enter it later
- The system itself does not stop working — it's just inaccessible until internet is restored

# 9. Daily Operations Guide

## A Complete Day in the Life

### Morning (8:00 AM - 10:00 AM)

**Manager arrives at the branch**

1. Log in to the system on the office computer
2. **Check the Dashboard:**
   - How many responses came in overnight?
   - Any new alerts (low ratings)?
   - What's the average rating for yesterday?
3. **Check alerts:**
   - If there are open alerts, read the customer comments
   - Acknowledge alerts you will handle
   - Assign alerts to team members if needed
4. **Review new responses:**
   - Read any comments left by customers
   - Note any recurring themes (e.g., "slow service" mentioned twice)

### Midday (10:00 AM - 2:00 PM)

**Customer traffic increases**

1. **Ensure QR codes are visible:**
   - Check that table cards are still in place
   - Check that the kiosk tablet (if used) is still working
2. **Monitor the dashboard:**
   - Responses come in as customers finish their meals
   - No action needed for positive feedback
   - Pay attention to any sudden negative responses

### Afternoon (2:00 PM - 5:00 PM)

**Lunch rush**

1. Many customers give feedback during this time
2. The dashboard updates automatically
3. If a low rating comes in, an alert is created immediately
4. Manager can respond right away if needed

## Common Problems and Solutions

---

### Problem: Cannot log in

**Possible causes:**
- Wrong email address
- Wrong password
- Account not yet activated
- Account was removed
- System is down

**Solutions:**

1. **Check your email:** Make sure you're using the correct email address
2. **Check your password:** Make sure you typed it correctly (passwords are case-sensitive)
3. **Try "Forgot password":** Click the "Forgot password" link, enter your email, and check for a password reset email
4. **Contact your admin:** If you still cannot log in, ask your organization admin to check your account
5. **Wait and retry:** If the system is down, try again later

---

### Problem: Cannot see any data on the dashboard

**Possible causes:**
- No responses yet in the system
- The date range filter is set to a time with no data
- You don't have permission to see the data
- An incorrect filter is applied

**Solutions:**

1. **Check the date filter:** Make sure it's not set to a future date or a very narrow range
2. **Reset all filters:** Remove any location, survey, or rating filters
3. **Check your role:** If you're a location manager, you only see your own location
4. **Submit a test response:** Scan your own QR code and submit a test to confirm data flows through

---

### Problem: Survey is not showing to customers

**Possible causes:**
- Survey is in Draft status (not published)
- Survey is Archived
- The public link is wrong
- QR code is damaged or unreadable

**Solutions:**

1. **Check survey status:** Go to Surveys and make sure the survey shows "Active" (green)
2. **Get the correct link:** Open the survey and copy the public link again
3. **Test the QR code:** Scan it with your own phone to make sure it works
4. **Reprint QR code:** If the QR code is damaged, download and print a fresh one

---

### Problem: A customer says the survey won't load on their phone

**Possible causes:**
- Customer's internet connection is slow
- Survey page is too large
- Customer's phone browser is outdated
- System is having a temporary issue

**Solutions:**

1. **Check the survey:** Open it on your own phone to verify it works
2. **Provide the direct link:** Have the customer type the URL instead of scanning QR
3. **Simplify:** If the survey has many questions, consider a simpler version
4. **Wait and retry:** Sometimes a quick refresh fixes the issue

---

### Problem: An alert was created but it's not relevant

**Possible causes:**
- The customer accidentally submitted a low rating
- The rating threshold is set too high
- The customer misunderstood the question

**Solutions:**

1. **Read the response:** Check what the customer actually said
2. **Dismiss the alert:** If it's not a real issue, click Dismiss
3. **Adjust the threshold:** If you're getting too many false alerts, consider lowering the threshold in Settings > Alert Configuration

---

### Problem: A response has an incorrect location

**Possible causes:**
- Customer scanned a QR code from a different location
- The survey was assigned to the wrong location

**Solutions:**

1. **Check the QR code:** Make sure the right QR code is at each location
2. **Check survey assignment:** Verify the survey is assigned to the correct location
3. **Note the discrepancy:** Make a note of the issue but don't delete the response (it's still valuable data)

**Possible causes:**

**Solutions:**

3. **Export in batches:** If you have a lot of data, use a narrower date range

**Solutions:**

# 12. Security

## How your data stays safe

### User Accounts

Each person who uses the system has their own account with:
- A unique email address
- A password (that only they know)
- A specific role (which determines what they can see and do)

**Why this matters:** If everyone shared one account, you couldn't tell who did what. With individual accounts:
- You know who viewed each response
- You know who changed each setting
- You know who acknowledged each alert
- You can remove access for specific people when they leave

### Passwords

- Passwords must be at least 12 characters long
- The system stores passwords in an encrypted format (it does NOT save the actual password)
- You should use a different password for this system than you use for other services
- You can change your password in Account Settings

### Permissions (Role-Based Access)

The most important security feature is that **people can only see what they need to see**.

**Example:** A location manager at Salmiya:
- CAN see responses from Salmiya ✅
- CANNOT see responses from Sharq ❌
- CAN acknowledge alerts about Salmiya ✅
- CANNOT delete the organization ❌
- CANNOT add new team members ❌

**Why this matters:** If a manager's account is compromised (someone else gets their password), the damage is limited to that one location.

### Data Isolation (Tenant Security)

Different organizations that use the system **cannot see each other's data**.

- If Restaurant A and Restaurant B both use the system, Restaurant A's admin can never see Restaurant B's responses
- The system enforces this at the database level (Row Level Security)
- Even if someone guesses another organization's survey URL, they cannot access the organization's dashboard

### Encryption

- All data sent between your browser and the system is encrypted (HTTPS)
- All data stored in the database is encrypted at rest
- File uploads (logos, evidence) are stored in secure, private storage

### Audit Logging

The system records every important action:
- Who logged in and when
- Who changed settings
- Who exported data
- Who added or removed team members

This creates an **audit trail** that can be reviewed if something suspicious happens.

### What the system does NOT store

- Customer names (feedback is anonymous)
- Customer personal information
- Payment information
- Passwords (only encrypted versions)

### Security Best Practices for Users

1. **Use strong passwords** — at least 12 characters, mix of letters, numbers, and symbols
2. **Don't share your password** — each person should have their own account
3. **Log out when you leave your computer** — especially on shared devices
4. **Report suspicious activity** — if you see data you shouldn't have access to, tell your admin
5. **Use a password manager** — it's easier and more secure than remembering passwords
